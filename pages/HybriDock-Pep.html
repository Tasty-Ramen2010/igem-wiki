01Overview

Background Information

Nearly all binder-based diagnostic tests rely on small molecules, binders that are designed to fit in grooves and pockets across proteins and create strong bonds between them. However, the small molecule-based binder tests can only detect a minority of all proteins, with the human body’s proteome only having 15% of its proteins be able to bind with small molecules. This percentage of proteins with plausible small molecules only grows smaller, as many diseases have begun to evolve out of them, an example being malaria’s HRP-II protein, already not found in more than 45 million infected people.

However, there does seem to be a solution: computationally designed de novo peptides–short chains of amino acids, generally under 25 amino acids long that are generated through computational methods and cannot be found in nature. These peptides are highly coveted as binders due to their variety coming from their enormous amount of possible combinations and their ability to bind to virtually any protein. Furthermore, with the vast amount of configurations, variable geometry and flexibility and sheer amount of peptide combinations, they are the perfect choice to create protein-specific, high-affinity binders, unique to each protein.

Personal Experience & Academic Feedback

The development, optimization, and computational modelling of peptide binders have always been gated by large corporations and prestigious universities due to their requirement of large computing clusters, along with slow, iterative computing algorithms, and an accuracy <10% for developed peptides. Furthermore, old Monte Carlo algorithms iterate through billions of different possible peptide poses, still unable to cover all poses.

During our work on peptides during the start, we relied on softwares like AlphaFold, ESMFold, Boltz-2, and we identified a key issue: the current software was incredibly unoptimized for peptides. Once we had our own doubts, we referred to academia to identify more problems regarding the computational development of peptides and it gave us shocking revelations:

“We had a team of like 12 people working on exactly the project (peptide development) you’re working on... after getting 12 people on it, our conclusion is it’s really hard to find data and most of it’s mislabeled” - Dr.Coventry

“...most of the softwares … aren't really super accurate to protein and peptide interactions. …under 30 amino acids, the accuracy of alpha fold dropped significantly ... ” - Dr.Hosseinzadeh

Through academic feedback and our own experience, we found the primary issue: there doesn’t exist a platform to effectively and accurately design peptide binders that include specialized docking and scoring.

Solution

In order to combat these issues, we made HybriDock-Pep over the course of 6 months. HybriDock-Pep is a novel ML-based docking and affinity scoring tool. We used a modified and improved upon version of RAPiDock as our base, using its features: bi-conformational graph encoding and SE(3)-equivariant denoising, along with our own custom GPU kernels and support for speed on x86 and ARM-based machines. Then, we paired it with our physics-based ML model to score protein-peptide interactions in kcal/mol with accuracy comparable to FEP, at a fraction of compute cost and an even smaller amount of time needed.
